# Triplepedia: The AI Wiki That Actually Checks Its Own Homework

**Every article written by AI. Every fact verified by our most powerful reasoning model. And now, you can interrogate any article in real time.**

---

## The internet has a knowledge problem

Wikipedia is one of humanity's greatest achievements. Seriously -- an encyclopedia maintained by millions of volunteers, available in 300+ languages, covering everything from quantum chromodynamics to the history of the sandwich. It's incredible.

But it has blind spots. Articles go stale. Edit wars produce bizarre compromises. Obscure topics get three sentences and a "citation needed" tag that stays there for six years. And the verification process, while rigorous in theory, relies on human volunteers who have day jobs and finite attention spans.

We thought: what if an AI could write encyclopedia-quality articles *and* fact-check them with the same rigor a team of editors would bring -- but do it in minutes instead of months?

That's Triplepedia.

## What Triplepedia actually is

Triplepedia is an AI-generated, AI-verified knowledge base built into Tripplet. Every article is structured like a proper encyclopedia entry: a clear summary, organized sections with subheadings, and an infobox with key facts. Think Wikipedia's layout, but cleaner and without the donation banners.

Here's where it gets interesting. Every single article passes through **Taipei 3.1 Extended** -- our most advanced reasoning model -- for fact-checking before it goes live. Taipei doesn't just skim the text. It uses extended thinking (up to 32K reasoning tokens) to cross-reference claims, flag inconsistencies, verify dates and figures, and catch the kind of subtle errors that slip past even careful human reviewers.

Each published article carries a timestamp showing exactly when it was last fact-checked and by which model. No ambiguity. No "trust us, it's accurate." You can see the receipts.

## Why AI-verified knowledge matters

Traditional wikis operate on an honor system backed by community moderation. That works remarkably well at scale, but it breaks down in predictable ways: low-traffic articles accumulate errors quietly, controversial topics become battlegrounds, and the gap between "published" and "verified" can stretch for years.

Triplepedia flips the model. Verification isn't an afterthought -- it's a mandatory step in the publishing pipeline. An article literally cannot reach "published" status without passing through Taipei's reasoning engine. Every claim gets scrutinized before a single reader sees it.

This doesn't make Triplepedia perfect. AI fact-checking has its own limitations, and we're transparent about that. But it does mean that every article meets a consistent, reproducible standard of verification -- something that's genuinely hard to achieve with volunteer editors alone.

## How it differs from Wikipedia

Let's be clear: Triplepedia isn't trying to replace Wikipedia. Wikipedia is a living monument to collaborative knowledge and it should exist forever. Triplepedia is something different.

**Speed.** A Triplepedia article can go from suggestion to published, fact-checked entry in minutes. Wikipedia's process -- drafting, sourcing, peer review, edit cycles -- can take weeks or months for complex topics.

**Consistency.** Every Triplepedia article follows the same structural template. No stylistic variation between "this article was written by an aerospace engineer" and "this article was written by a enthusiastic undergraduate at 2 AM."

**Verification transparency.** You know exactly when an article was checked and by what model. Wikipedia's revision history is public but dense -- figuring out which claims have been verified requires archaeology.

**Interactivity.** And this is the big one.

## "Ask about this article" -- your new favorite button

Starting today, every Triplepedia article has a docked chat panel. Hit the "Ask about this article" button in the top bar, and a panel slides in from the right side of the screen. From there, you can ask questions about anything in the article -- and the AI answers in real time, grounded in the article's content.

Reading about black holes and wondering what "spaghettification" actually feels like? Ask. Scanning an article on the Roman Empire and curious how the road system compared to modern highways? Ask. The chat panel knows the full context of the article you're reading, so answers are specific and relevant -- not generic web search results.

This turns Triplepedia from a static reference into a conversation. You're not just reading knowledge; you're *exploring* it, following your curiosity wherever it goes.

Triplepedia is in v0.1 and growing fast. Anyone can suggest new articles, and we're expanding the knowledge base daily. Coming soon: article revision history, community voting on suggested topics, and the ability to request deeper coverage on specific sections.

Knowledge should be accessible, accurate, and alive. Triplepedia is our attempt to make all three happen at once.
